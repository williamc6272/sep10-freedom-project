# Entry 7
##### 6/8/26
## Content 
#### In Class Presentation
From my in class presentation, I learned several important communication skills. One of the biggest takeaways was the importance of making eye contact with the audience. This helped me stay engaged with listeners and made my presentation feel more confident and natural. I also learned to project my voice so that everyone in the room could clearly hear and understand what I was saying. Another key lesson was learning how to focus only on the most important parts of my project. Since my project included a lot of information about forensic science technologies, pricing, and future innovations, I had to choose what to highlight instead of explaining everything in detail. This helped me keep my presentation clear and easier to follow.
#### Expo  elevator pitch
During my Expo elevator pitch, I presented my project to judges, which was a more fast-paced and high pressure experience. I learned how to stay clear and focused while managing my time carefully. Since the pitch was short, I had to quickly communicate the main idea of my project without going into too much detail. I also learned how to explain technical parts of my website, including Bootstrap components, in a simple way. Being able to describe tools like the navbar, accordion, and cards helped me show how my website was structured and how it supports my research on forensic science technology. This experience improved my ability to communicate technical information under pressure.
## EDP
I followed the engineering design process by first identifying the problem that forensic science technologies are expensive, resource-heavy, and not always accessible to smaller labs. Next, I researched existing tools such as DNA analysis systems, fingerprint databases, digital forensics tools, and PCR equipment, along with their costs and limitations. After that, I brainstormed future solutions, including nano-tech robots for evidence reconstruction and quantum computing to improve speed and accuracy in forensic analysis. I then designed and built a [website](https://williamc6272.github.io/sep10-freedom-project/) using Bootstrap components like a navbar, accordion, and cards to organize my research clearly. I also used [A-Frame](https://github.com/williamc6272/sep10-freedom-project/blob/main/aframe-tinkering-1.html) to create a quantum computer simulation, which visually represents how advanced computing could improve forensic investigations. Finally, I tested my project by presenting it in class and at the Expo, which helped me improve how clearly I communicated my ideas.
